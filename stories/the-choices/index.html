How we buildOn purpose

We chose the place the work already lives.

The products look different because the days are different. The choices underneath are the same: one record, a person on anything that leaves the building, and a public account of how we check the software.

This page is how we build. It is not a quote from a named customer, and it is not a measured saving.

Where

The software meets the work. The work does not move first.

  • In the chat

    Tradey runs where a food trade is already agreed, on WhatsApp. The offer is drafted there. A person approves it before it is sent.

  • On site

    Workslip takes the checklist and the photos where the job is. The office approves that same record.

  • On the shipment

    Shippy creates the shipment once. Documents, customs and status stay with it, including what arrived by email.

Who decides

The system prepares. A person decides.

That is the rule on Tradey, and it is the shape of Workslip's approval. An assistant can answer from your own data. It does not give legal or financial advice, and Tradey does not buy or sell currency. It shows the rate.

  • One core, fitted

    Traders share a core, so a system designed around one workflow does not start from zero. The flows and the connections are still built for that house.

  • A pilot before live

    Tradey is used for real with approval on everything, and daily fixes, before normal operation. A larger trader can start with one department.

  • Beside what you run

    The products sit with the ERP, the accounts, the carrier and the files you already have. Data is entered once. We confirm what is supported today before we promise a connection.

How we show it

We publish the check, including the gaps.

Security, the way we validate our own work, system status and the roadmap are public. The two Workslip figures on the front page are labelled for what they are: time through the product, not a saving. A story on this site says which product it belongs to, and whether that product is live, a demo, or still in development.
